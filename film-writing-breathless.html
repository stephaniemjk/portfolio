French New Wave · Jean-Luc Godard

Breathless (1960)

This is such a subversion of the usual noir films that we see. Michel is not those tight-lipped, dark and brooding leads that we’re used to, and instead he’s kind of an endearing loser. His behavior’s acceptable, despite for instance stealing money from his ex-girlfriend, because Belmondo exudes so much physical comedy. We have this femme fatale-like character that betrays him at the end, except to paraphrase Michel, she’s not that pretty. Instead of following the storyline of running away from the cops, there are some secondary storylines like comically trying to reach Antonio to get Michel’s money, Patricia trying to be a journalist girl boss, or Michel and Patricia discussing the validity of their connection.

Jean Lucy Godard uses dysnarrative techniques, like jump cuts or misaligning audio and images, to distort the audience’s perception of time and continuity. There’s a scene in which Michel’s mad at Patricia and he’s driving and he’s saying how a girl has a pretty face, pretty knees, and there’s a new shot of a different road or pathway with each iteration, and the audience is left to wonder how long he’s been mumbling this and subjecting Patricia to his diatribe. It’s almost hilarious in a way because Michel rambles a lot. In these kind of noir films, the audience is immerse into the emotional world of the characters, and Godard consistently tries to remind you that you’re watching a film- whether Michel breaks the fourth wall in the beginning or we see LED billboards of Michel’s imminent arrest. Everything is deliberately contrived, subsequently making it harder for me to get emotionally invested in the story at all.

There’s also this underlying pessimistic sentiment about modern women. We have this American woman who is somewhat proficient in French as the lead, and her inability to fully grasp the language and share similar interests with Michel makes the audience doubt the strength of their romantic connection. Additionally, it’s kind of hilarious that she’s a journalist and only found out nearly the last act of the film about Michel’s reveal as a fugitive. She says one thing but does another - like she doesn’t want to be a model because of the casting couch culture (understandably) but then she also kisses the journalist giving her a job. She turns Michel in because that proves she doesn’t love him, but then at the same time she stays with him til he dies. Michel has been more dutiful to Patricia than she has to him, and she embodies that complicated, existential characterization that we see often amongst nouvelle vague characters. I’m just conjecturing, but I wonder if this characterization is subverting women’s flat characters in media and films previously, and if there’s a relationship between fleshing out female characters and pessimism around them.
