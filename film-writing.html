Whit Stillman

  • The Last Days of Disco (1998)

    This film is similar to its predecessors, in the way it covers a different era of this affluent American class, whether they’re UHPs in Metropolitan, Facha Americanas in Barcelona, or scum yuppies in The Last Days of Disco.

  • Barcelona (1994)

    Whit Stillman is able to use dialogue to demarcate time rather than using obvious, visual settings.

French New Wave

  • Elevator to the Gallows (1958)

    Louis Malle

    I really enjoyed Elevator to the Gallows because it feels like a deconstructed film noir and it traverses different genres.

  • The Lovers (1958)

    Louis Malle

    In this film, I was really impressed by the visuals - the costumes, the camera shots, the lighting, and the setting to convey its message about the need for Jeanne to constantly perform her womanly duties and her inability to connect with herself.

  • The Cousins (1959)

    Claude Chabrol

    It’s interesting watching Le Beau Serge and Les Cousins back to back because with the first film, I thought Chabrol was sooo elitist.

  • Breathless (1960)

    Jean-Luc Godard

    This is such a subversion of the usual noir films that we see.

  • Vivre Sa Vie (1962)

    Jean-Luc Godard

    Godard distances the audience from the character and the plot by having time lapses, unclear narrative flow, and constrained cinematography.

  • Masculin Féminin (1966)

    Jean-Luc Godard

    The crux of this film is about the mismatched romance between Paul and Madeleine.

  • Two English Girls (1971)

    François Truffaut

    I think Two English Girls and The 400 Blows are my favorite Truffaut films by far.
